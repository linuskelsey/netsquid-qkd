# kubo-2011-PRL

Kubo, Grezes et al. (CEA Saclay/Quantronics group), published in Physical Review Letters 107, 220501 (2011), reported the first proof-of-concept quantum memory transfer between a superconducting transmon qubit and an NV-centre spin ensemble. An NV ensemble (~10^11 centres) was coupled to a transmon qubit via a frequency-tunable superconducting resonator bus, with state transfer implemented using an adiabatic SWAP (aSWAP) gate. A single qubit excitation was stored at τs,III=65 ns and retrieved at τr,III=116 ns for group III NV centres. The retrieved fidelity was 0.14 (group III) and 0.07 (group I), strongly limited by dephasing from the 14N hyperfine structure of NV centres, which produces 2.2 MHz-separated peaks causing rapid decoherence (~200 ns Ramsey damping corresponding to 1.6 MHz homogeneous linewidth). A coherent superposition was stored and retrieved, though with amplitude reduced to approximately one fifth of the input due to hyperfine dephasing. Ramsey-like experiments at single-photon level confirmed coherent dynamics during storage. Despite the low fidelity, this experiment established the key principle that a superconducting qubit state can be mapped onto and retrieved from a spin ensemble quantum memory, motivating subsequent work to suppress hyperfine-induced dephasing and improve overall memory efficiency.
