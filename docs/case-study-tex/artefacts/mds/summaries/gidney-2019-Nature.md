# gidney-2019-Nature (Gidney & Ekerå 2021 — Quantum journal)

Craig Gidney and Martin Ekerå, "How to factor 2048 bit RSA integers in 8 hours using 20 million noisy qubits," Quantum 5, 433 (2021). The paper combines optimisations from Shor's algorithm, Ekerå-Håstad's discrete-logarithm variant, Zalka's coset representation, windowed arithmetic, and reaction-limited carry runways to dramatically reduce the cost of quantum integer factorisation. Under physically motivated assumptions for large-scale superconducting qubit platforms (planar nearest-neighbour grid, 10^-3 physical gate error rate, 1 µs surface code cycle time, 10 µs reaction time), the parallel construction factors a 2048-bit RSA integer using 20 million physical qubits in approximately 8 hours — a reduction in spacetime volume of over 100× compared to prior estimates (e.g. Fowler et al. 2012 required ~1000 million qubit-days). In the abstract circuit model the construction uses 3n + 0.002n·lg(n) logical qubits. This is the primary reference for the "standard architecture" qubit cost of breaking RSA-2048, and serves as the baseline against which Gouzien & Sangouard 2021 demonstrate a three-orders-of-magnitude reduction in physical qubit count when a multimode quantum memory is added.
