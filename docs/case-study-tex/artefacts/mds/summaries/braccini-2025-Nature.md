# braccini-2025-Nature

Braccini et al. (UCL, Bose group, 2025) theoretically proposed and experimentally validated a method to detect nonclassical correlations in macroscopic qubit ensembles via parity-measurement-induced disturbance (NDC violation). Dispersive coupling of N qubits to a microwave resonator followed by homodyne measurement implements an effective parity readout; violation of the No Disturbance Condition (NDC) certifies nonclassicality without requiring full state tomography. In the ideal case, the NDC violation is constant with ensemble size N. With realistic noise — decoherence, inhomogeneous broadening, and measurement imperfections — a quantum-to-classical transition occurs and violation becomes undetectable above a critical ensemble size N_NDC. Modelling three platforms, detectable violation was predicted up to N_NDC≈41 for superconducting qubits, ≈53 for Rydberg atoms, and ≈110 for spin qubits with spin-orbit coupling. IBM Quantum Circuit experiments detected NDC violation in ensembles of up to 38 qubits, consistent with predictions. The work provides a scalable, measurement-based witness of macroscopic quantum coherence, relevant to benchmarking spin-ensemble quantum memories and hybrid quantum systems where many-body nonclassicality must be preserved during storage and retrieval operations.
