# unknown-2026-unknown (Hogan 2026 — UCL PhD thesis)

Patrick Hogan, "A Unit Cooperativity Random Access Quantum Memory," PhD thesis, University College London, May 2026. The thesis targets the central bottleneck in spin-ensemble quantum memories — efficiency at the storage-retrieval interface — by making measurements at the unit cooperativity point, where energy transfer between ensemble and resonator becomes lossless. Two spin systems are studied: bismuth donors in silicon (Bi:Si) and ytterbium ions in yttrium orthosilicate (Yb:YSO). For Bi:Si, an on-chip waveguide is used to drive nuclear spin transitions, enabling ENDOR spectroscopy at millikelvin temperatures and hyperpolarisation of the nuclear spin state beyond Boltzmann equilibrium; nuclear spin T1 is measured via hyperpolarisation decay. For Yb:YSO, storage and retrieval at unit cooperativity (C = 1) is demonstrated, showing minimal resonator-ensemble losses. Despite achieving C = 1 for the first time in a spin-ensemble memory, overall system efficiency remains low due to losses elsewhere in the chain; the thesis provides quantitative analysis identifying each loss mechanism and adjustments required to exceed 90% fidelity. A proposal for spatially resolved random-access quantum memory using magnetic field gradients is also presented. This is the most current result from UCL's Quantum Spin Dynamics group and represents the state of the art in cooperativity for solid-state microwave quantum memories.
