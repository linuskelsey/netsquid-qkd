# alexander-2022-unknown

Alexander et al. (UCL/Morton group, 2022) studied coherent spin dynamics of rare-earth ions doped into Y2SiO5 crystals in the high-cooperativity regime, coupling 145Nd3+ and natYb3+/171Yb3+ spin ensembles to planar NbN superconducting microresonators. Cooperativity ranged from C=4 to C=245. For 145Nd3+, the Hahn echo coherence time T2 increased from 24(1) µs at 1.2 K to 0.41(1) ms at 14 mK; for natYb3+, T2 rose from 33(7) µs to 3.4(1) ms at millikelvin temperatures. Isotopically pure 171Yb3+ measured at the zero-field clock transition achieved T2=6(1) ms, approaching the 3.48 ms predicted limit set by the 89Y nuclear spin bath via CCE calculations. Two complementary decoherence mitigation strategies were identified: orienting the field to minimise the effective g-factor (suppressing nuclear-bath-induced flip-flops), and operating at the zero-field clock transition where first-order field sensitivity vanishes. This work established that rare-earth spin ensembles coupled to superconducting resonators can achieve millisecond-scale electron spin coherence at millikelvin temperatures, directly relevant to quantum memory applications for superconducting quantum processors.
