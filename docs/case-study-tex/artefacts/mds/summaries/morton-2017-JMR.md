# morton-2017-JMR

Morton and Bertet (UCL/CEA, review), published in Journal of Magnetic Resonance 287, 128–139 (2018), surveyed the landscape of spin systems for quantum information storage and high-sensitivity ESR. Molecular spins were reviewed, including N@C60 with T2=240 µs at 170 K and vanadium complexes with T2=700 µs at 10 K. Donor spins in silicon were highlighted, particularly bismuth donors at the clock transition in 28Si achieving T2=2.7 s at 4.3 K with a simple Hahn echo, the longest electron spin coherence time then measured. NV centres in diamond reached T2~ms at room temperature using CPMG sequences. Rare-earth ions in optical crystals were reviewed, with electron spin T2 values up to ~100 µs and nuclear spin T2 values up to 6 hours. The cooperativity C=g_ens²/(κγ) was identified as the key figure of merit for spin-ensemble quantum memories coupled to superconducting microresonators, with C≥1 required for efficient storage and retrieval. High-sensitivity ESR using bismuth donors in silicon coupled to a superconducting resonator and Josephson parametric amplifier reached a detection limit of 65 spins per √Hz, illustrating the dual relevance of these systems for both quantum memory and ultra-sensitive magnetic resonance spectroscopy.
