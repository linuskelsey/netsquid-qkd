# lenz-2021-AdvMat

Lenz, König, Hunger and van Slageren (Univ. Stuttgart), published in Advanced Materials 33, 2101673 (2021), demonstrated room-temperature quantum memories using a molecular organic radical spin ensemble in a microwave resonator. BDPA (α,γ-bisdiphenylene-β-phenylallyl benzene solvate), approximately 6×10^18 spins in 9.4 mg, was placed in a copper semi-confocal Fabry-Pérot resonator operating at 35 GHz (Q-band). Vacuum Rabi oscillations were observed with an effective collective coupling frequency of Ω_eff/2π=78 MHz. The phase memory time from spin echo was TM=1.4(1) µs at 7 K, far exceeding the bare BDPA coherence (~100 ns at 77 K–RT), with TM=600 ns remaining accessible at room temperature. Echo modulation patterns revealed polariton-mode coherence mediated by residual impurity spins (~10% of the ensemble). Three microwave pulses with encoded phase information were stored and retrieved at a storage time of 2.8 µs with faithful phase reproduction. This constituted the first time-domain microwave pulse storage and retrieval at room temperature using a molecular spin ensemble, demonstrating that chemically synthesised molecular spins represent a practical and synthetically tuneable platform for room-temperature multimode quantum memories, with direct relevance to quantum repeater architectures not requiring cryogenic operation.
