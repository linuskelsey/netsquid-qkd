# wen-2025-unknown

Wen, Ranjan et al. (Quantronics CEA and ENS Lyon, 2025) demonstrated a frequency- and bandwidth-tunable superconducting resonator for addressing spin ensembles at their clock transition. A 50 nm NbTiN film on Si formed a high-kinetic-inductance microresonator with an 800 nm-wide inductive microwire; DC current through this wire A tuned the resonator frequency by ±80 MHz. Bandwidth was tuned independently via three-wave mixing coupling to a buffer resonator B through a 250 nm-wide kinetic inductance nanowire. Bismuth donors in 28Si-enriched silicon (flip-chip) were biased at the clock transition at 25.6 mT / 7.3382 GHz. At the clock transition, T1=53 s (Purcell regime) and T2=450 ms (Hahn echo) were measured. ESR linewidth was ~300 kHz at 2.1 mT field. Echo silencing was demonstrated by rapidly detuning the resonator frequency away from the spin resonance. ENDOR spectroscopy resolved NMR-like transitions at ~37 MHz (Bi hyperfine transitions), showing 6 dips in the echo spectrum. The ability to tune both resonator frequency and bandwidth independently enables optimised absorption and emission protocols, directly addressing the engineering requirements identified by quantum cascaded models of spin-ensemble microwave memories.
