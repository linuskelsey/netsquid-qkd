# wolfowicz-2013-NatNano

Wolfowicz, Tyryshkin et al. (UCL/Princeton/Morton group), published in Nature Nanotechnology 8, 561–564 (2013), discovered and characterised atomic clock transitions in bismuth donor spins in silicon, achieving seconds-long electron spin coherence in a solid-state system. ESR-type clock transitions, where the transition frequency is first-order insensitive to magnetic field, were found at 7.0317 GHz / 79.8 mT for Bi donors. In isotopically purified 28Si (3.6×10^14 cm^-3 Bi), a simple two-pulse Hahn echo at 4.3 K yielded T2=2.7 s, the longest electron spin coherence time yet demonstrated in a solid at that time. In natural Si at the clock transition, T2=93 ms, versus less than 1 ms away from the clock transition. The hyperfine constant was precisely determined: A=1.475169(7) GHz. Three decoherence mechanisms were separated quantitatively: instantaneous diffusion (ID, scaling quadratically with |df/dB|), indirect flip-flops (iFF, linear), and direct flip-flops (dFF, concentration-limited). The clock transition suppresses ID and iFF, leaving only dFF as the fundamental limit at low density. This work established the Bi:Si system as the leading electron spin quantum memory platform and introduced atomic clock transitions as the central engineering principle for maximising electron spin coherence in semiconductor and other solid-state hosts.
