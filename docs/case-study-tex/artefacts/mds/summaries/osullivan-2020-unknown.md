# osullivan-2020-unknown

O'Sullivan, Kennedy et al. (UCL/Morton group, 2020) characterised ESR linewidths of bismuth donors in silicon coupled to planar Nb superconducting microresonators, identifying strain as the dominant broadening mechanism and charting a path to unit cooperativity. Bi donors were implanted in natural Si, with Nb resonators patterned directly on the crystal surface. At the 6.76 GHz clock transition, the minimum ESR linewidth was ~1.25 MHz, approximately half that obtained with Al resonators (the Nb-on-Si configuration generates less strain). In a strain-free 3D cavity geometry, the linewidth dropped to 0.5 MHz at the clock transition. Linewidth scaled linearly with |df/dB| across transitions, implying ~0.5 mT effective field inhomogeneity from 29Si nuclear spins. Cooperativity reached C~0.2 under these conditions. T1 entered the Purcell-enhanced regime at low temperatures. Power-dependent spectroscopy resolved spatial strain distributions induced by the metal film. The route to unit cooperativity was identified as requiring higher Bi implantation dose, isotopic purification of the Si host to remove 29Si, and reduced strain from the resonator fabrication process. This paper established the key experimental and materials constraints for building a Bi:Si-based microwave quantum memory with sufficient cooperativity for quantum-limited operation.
