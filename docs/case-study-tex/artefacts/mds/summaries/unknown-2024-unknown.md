# unknown-2024-unknown (Hamamoto et al. 2024 — OIST/Kubo group)

Hamamoto, Bhunia, Bhattacharya, Takahashi and Kubo (OIST, Japan), preprint dated March 2024. The paper demonstrates a dielectric microwave resonator based on rutile (TiO2) designed for spin-based quantum devices requiring both high quality factor and large optical access — requirements normally in tension. Rutile's high dielectric constant (ε⊥≈130, ε∥≈255 at 4 K) confines the microwave electric field inside the crystal, making enclosure-wall dissipation negligible and enabling an internal quality factor Q_int = 2.30×10^4 with 8 mm diameter optical apertures at both 4 K and 10 mK. Numerical simulations show the AC magnetic field is homogeneous over ~95% of the sample space, with ~37% of magnetic energy inside the rutile, and predict Q_int exceeding 10^6 by extending the enclosure height. The single-spin coupling constant is g_single/2π ≈ 0.07 Hz, comparable to other 3D resonators. ESR spectroscopy on DPPH (high-cooperativity regime) and P1 centres in diamond (strong coupling regime) was demonstrated in a dilution refrigerator, including pulsed T1 and T2 measurements. The relevance is primarily to microwave-to-optical quantum transduction, where spin ensembles (e.g. Er³⁺) must be simultaneously addressable by microwave resonator and optical beam — a requirement that planar superconducting resonators cannot satisfy.
