# dyte-2025-NatComms

Dyte, Manna, Covre da Silva, Rastelli and Chekhovich, published in Nature Communications 17:239 (2026), reported storing quantum coherence in a quantum dot nuclear spin ensemble for over 100 milliseconds. A single GaAs/AlGaAs quantum dot with approximately 5×10^4 nuclear spins (75As, 69Ga, 71Ga, spin-3/2) was studied; elastic strain isolated the Iz=±1/2 central transition of 75As with a linewidth of ~0.8 kHz, while the first-order quadrupolar splitting was νQ(1)≈255.1 kHz at Bz≈5.16 T. The FID coherence time was T2*≈0.46 ms and the Hahn echo time T2^HE≈1.38 ms. CHASE-40 dynamical decoupling (40 π-pulses per cycle, 48 cycles) extended T2 to 106.6 ms, with a maximum spin memory time TM≈136 ms at cycle time TCycle≈2 ms. Residual decoheherence arose from three-body nuclear spin interactions not cancelled by pairwise decoupling. Coherence decay under CHASE-40 was shown to be uniform across arbitrary initial spin states, confirming applicability to quantum superpositions including single-quantum magnon states. This constituted a record coherence time for semiconductor quantum dot nuclear spins and demonstrated that this platform can serve as a long-lived quantum memory register compatible with optically addressable spin qubits in III-V quantum dot devices.
