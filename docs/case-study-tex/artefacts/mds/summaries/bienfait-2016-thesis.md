# bienfait-2016-thesis (Bienfait 2016 — Paris-Saclay PhD thesis)

Alexia Bienfait, PhD thesis, Université Paris-Saclay / CEA Quantronics group (Patrice Bertet), 2016. The thesis applies circuit-QED concepts to electron spin resonance in order to perform magnetic resonance experiments in a regime where quantum fluctuations of the microwave field dominate. Bismuth donors in silicon (Si:Bi) are coupled to high-Q, small-mode-volume superconducting planar resonators at millikelvin temperatures, with a Josephson parametric amplifier (JPA) providing near-quantum-limited amplification of the spin signal. A detection sensitivity of 2000 spins per experimental sequence is demonstrated — a four-orders-of-magnitude improvement over the state of the art. The Purcell effect is observed for electron spins for the first time: coupling spins to a resonator with enhanced electromagnetic density of states accelerates spontaneous spin relaxation (T1 reduced from seconds to milliseconds), providing a route to fast spin reset. A squeezing-enhanced ESR demonstration shows noise reduction below the quantum shot-noise limit using squeezed microwave states from a flux-pumped JPA. Together, these results establish the theoretical and experimental infrastructure — quantum-limited readout, Purcell enhancement, cooperativity formalism — that directly underpins all subsequent spin-ensemble quantum memory work including Ranjan 2020, O'Sullivan 2022, and Hogan 2026.
