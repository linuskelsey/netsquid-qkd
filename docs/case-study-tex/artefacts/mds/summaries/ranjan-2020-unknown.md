# ranjan-2020-unknown

Ranjan, O'Sullivan et al. (CEA Saclay and UCL), published in Physical Review Letters 125, 210505 (2020), demonstrated multimode storage of quantum microwave fields in bismuth donor spins in silicon for over 100 ms. Bi donors in a 700 nm epilayer of 28Si-enriched silicon were coupled to an Al superconducting resonator (7.336 GHz) at 20 mK, biased at the clock transition at 27 mT where decoherence from 29Si is suppressed. A train of 20 weak Gaussian microwave pulses (n_in=240 or 24 photons per pulse) was stored and retrieved via a single π refocusing pulse, with phase coherence preserved across all 20 modes. Retrieved intensity was 0.3±0.1 photons (for 240-photon input) and 0.03±0.01 photons (for 24-photon input), confirming operation in the quantum regime. Output noise statistics were σ_O≈σ_E=1.5±0.3 photons, consistent with the quantum noise limit (ideal value n_id=3.5±1.7 from model). Explicit measurement confirmed T2E>100 ms. Cooperativity C≈0.033 at the clock transition. The work provided the first demonstration of multimode storage and retrieval of quantum-level microwave fields in a spin ensemble, with the 100 ms storage time representing a benchmark relevant to quantum repeater memory requirements.
